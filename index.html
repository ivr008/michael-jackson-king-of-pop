The Life

A Once-in-a-Century Talent

Michael Joseph Jackson was born on August 29, 1958, in Gary, Indiana — the eighth of ten children. From the moment he took the microphone as a small boy fronting his brothers, it was clear he was different: a singer with the timing of a seasoned professional and a performer who seemed to feel every note.

As the lead voice of the Jackson 5 he became a child star, and as a solo artist he became a phenomenon. Working with producer Quincy Jones, he released a run of albums — Off the Wall, Thriller and Bad — that reshaped pop music and made the music video a serious art form. Thriller remains the best-selling album of all time.

He was a dancer of extraordinary invention, refining steps like the moonwalk into global shorthand for genius. He was a perfectionist in the studio, a pioneer of spectacle on stage, and a philanthropist whose charity work touched children across the world. He was, and remains, the definitive pop icon — the benchmark against which everything since is measured.

His death on June 25, 2009, at the age of 50, prompted an outpouring of grief unmatched in the history of popular culture. More than a decade later, his music is still streamed billions of times a year by fans old and new.

Over the Years

His Life & Career, Decade by Decade

From a six-year-old frontman in Gary, Indiana to the biggest star on the planet — the moments that built the legend.

1958

A Star Is Born

Michael Joseph Jackson is born on August 29 in Gary, Indiana — the eighth of ten children in a working-class family. By age five he is already singing in front of anyone who will listen.

1964

The Jackson 5

Michael joins brothers Jackie, Tito, Jermaine and Marlon in a family band that storms local talent shows across the Midwest. His voice — impossibly soulful for a child — makes him the obvious frontman.

1969

Motown & “I Want You Back”

Signed to Motown, the Jackson 5's debut single rockets to No. 1. An 11-year-old Michael is suddenly a national phenomenon.

1972

Going Solo

While still with his brothers, Michael releases solo singles — “Got to Be There” and “Ben,” the latter his first solo No. 1 hit.

1978

The Wiz

Michael stars as the Scarecrow in The Wiz and meets composer-producer Quincy Jones. The collaboration that follows will reshape popular music.

1979

Off the Wall

With Quincy Jones producing, Off the Wall sells roughly 20 million copies and turns Michael into a solo superstar — and the first solo artist to land four Top-10 singles from one album.

1982

Thriller

Released November 30, Thriller will become the best-selling album of all time, blending pop, rock, funk and disco behind “Billie Jean,” “Beat It” and the title track.

1983

The Moonwalk

On the Motown 25 television special, Michael performs “Billie Jean” and debuts the moonwalk. It is instantly the most talked-about TV performance in pop history.

1983

Thriller’s Short Film

John Landis directs the 14-minute “Thriller” film — a horror-movie spectacle that makes the music video an art form and sets a new bar for the medium.

1984

Eight Grammys & the Pepsi Accident

Thriller wins a record-breaking eight Grammy Awards in a single night. Days earlier, pyrotechnics set his hair alight during a Pepsi commercial shoot — an accident that deepens his legend.

1984

The Victory Tour

Michael tours North America with his brothers one final time, playing to packed stadiums before an estimated two million fans.

1985

“We Are the World”

Michael co-writes (with Lionel Richie) and performs on the USA for Africa charity anthem, assembling dozens of stars and raising millions for famine relief.

1987

Bad

The album Bad arrives with a Martin Scorsese-directed title video, and Michael launches his first-ever solo world tour.

1988

The Bad World Tour

The Bad World Tour plays 123 shows to about 4.4 million people, including a record seven sold-out nights at Wembley Stadium. Michael also buys Neverland Ranch.

1990

A White House Visit

Michael is honoured at the White House as “Artist of the Decade” for his charitable work, a symbol of his extraordinary cultural reach.

1991

Dangerous

Released November 26, Dangerous delivers “Black or White,” “Remember the Time” and “In the Closet,” and reasserts Michael as pop's most ambitious innovator.

1992

The Dangerous World Tour

The Dangerous World Tour opens, and his Bucharest concert is broadcast live to dozens of countries — one of the most-watched concerts ever televised.

1993

Super Bowl XXVII

Michael's halftime show at the Rose Bowl becomes one of the most-watched musical performances in American television history, ending with “Heal the World.”

1997

HIStory World Tour

Following the 1995 HIStory album, the HIStory World Tour becomes the highest-attended concert tour by a solo artist, drawing around 4.5 million people.

1999

MJ & Friends

Michael headlines charity concerts in Seoul and Munich, joined on stage by guests including Slash, raising funds for children's causes around the world.

2001

Invincible & 30 Years

Invincible is released, and two star-studded concerts at Madison Square Garden celebrate 30 years of Michael Jackson as a solo artist.

2005

Acquitted

After a 14-week trial that dominates headlines worldwide, Michael is acquitted of all charges and vows to focus again on music and his family.

2009

This Is It

Michael announces 50 sold-out shows at London's O2 Arena under the banner “This Is It.” Rehearsals promise a spectacular return to the stage.

2009

June 25

On June 25, Michael Jackson dies at his Los Angeles home at the age of 50. Fans across the globe gather to mourn the loss of the King of Pop.

2009

The Legacy

A star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, a farewell film (This Is It), and countless tributes follow — and his music keeps reaching new generations every year.

Famous Concerts & Tours

The performances that redefined what a concert could be — and the tours that broke attendance records around the world.

1983

Motown 25

Pasadena Civic Auditorium, USA

The night the moonwalk was born. Michael's “Billie Jean” performance turned a TV anniversary special into the most famous few minutes in pop.

1984

Victory Tour

North America · 55 shows

A final stadium run with the Jacksons, seen by roughly two million people and cementing Michael's drawing power as a live act.

1987–89

Bad World Tour

Worldwide · 123 shows

His first solo tour and, at the time, the biggest and highest-grossing concert tour ever undertaken by a solo artist.

1992–93

Dangerous World Tour

Worldwide · 69 shows

A theatrical blockbuster. The Bucharest show was broadcast live worldwide and remains one of the most celebrated concert films of all time.

1993

Super Bowl XXVII Halftime

Rose Bowl, USA

A jaw-dropping medley capped by “Heal the World.” The performance helped make the Super Bowl halftime show the cultural event it is today.

1996–97

HIStory World Tour

Worldwide · 82 shows

The highest-attended tour of his career and by any solo artist, playing to around 4.5 million fans across five continents.

1999

MJ & Friends

Seoul & Munich

Two enormous charity concerts for children's charities, with a guest list that included Slash and a host of international stars.

2001

30th Anniversary Special

Madison Square Garden, USA

Two nights honouring three decades of solo artistry, with surprise appearances from friends and peers across the music world.

2009

This Is It

The O2 Arena, London · 50 shows

A planned comeback residency that sold out 50 dates in minutes. Though it never happened, the rehearsals became the acclaimed film This Is It.

The Legacy

Michael Jackson changed the sound of pop, the look of the music video, and the scale of live performance. He made dances that the whole world learned, videos that played like cinema, and songs that still fill dance floors more than forty years after they were written.

Since his passing, his influence has only grown. The concert film This Is It became one of the highest-grossing documentaries ever released; Cirque du Soleil built two hit shows around his catalogue; and the Broadway musical MJ brought his story to a new generation of theatregoers. Posthumous albums like Michael and Xscape topped charts worldwide.

Inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame and honoured with a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, he is remembered not only as the King of Pop, but as one of the most influential artists in the history of recorded music. The moonwalk, the glove, the fedora — they belong to him forever.
